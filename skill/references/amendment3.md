# Amendment 3 (2026): what the text says

Source: enrolled CS/HJR 1-F (2026 Special Session F), approved by voters November 3, 2026. Effective January 1, 2027 (new Art. XII schedule section). The implementing bill is CS/SB 4-F (Ch. 2026-240, Laws of Fla.).
Text checked: 2026-11-04 against the enrolled joint resolution (flsenate.gov/Session/Bill/2026F/1F/BillText/er/PDF).

Quote the constitution, not the ballot summary. They differ in places (see §4).

## 1. The exemption (Art. VII, § 6(a))

| Item | Text / effect |
|---|---|
| Homestead, school levies | Up to $25,000 (unchanged). |
| Homestead, all other levies | "Beginning on January 1, 2027, up to the assessed valuation of one hundred and fifty thousand dollars." "Beginning on January 1, 2028, up to the assessed valuation of two hundred and fifty thousand dollars." § 6(a)(1)a.2. |
| Structure | One exemption "up to" the cap, applied from the first dollar of assessed value. It replaces the current $25,000 + additional exemption (up to $26,411 in 2026, applied to value between $50,000 and $75,000+) for non-school levies. |
| Inflation | The $250,000 is indexed to CPI-U beginning January 1, 2029, when the change is positive. § 6(a)(2)a. |
| New residents | A person who "had not maintained a permanent residence in this state as of December 31, 2026" gets $50,000 for non-school levies, indexed from January 1, 2028, moving to the full amount "beginning with the fifth year of exemption." § 6(a)(1)b., (2)b. |
| Local shortening of the 5-year wait | From January 1, 2030, a county or municipality may shorten the five-year wait "for a critical local need" by a two-thirds vote of the governing body's membership. § 6(a)(4)a.2. |
| Local option to exempt more | The Legislature "shall, by general law, prescribe a uniform procedure for counties and municipalities, for their respective levies, to increase the amount of assessed valuation exempt from taxation … up to all remaining assessed valuation." § 6(a)(4)a.1. Special districts may raise their own exemption by referendum. § 6(a)(4)b. |
| **Special assessments** | Exemption is "from taxation thereon, **except assessments for special benefits**." § 6(a)(1)a. and b. Special assessments are therefore outside the exemption. Homesteads pay them in full. |

## 2. Limit on the use of ad valorem revenue (Art. VII, § 9(a)(2)), counties and municipalities only

"Ad valorem taxes levied by counties and municipalities shall be used only to:
a. Provide for public safety, including law enforcement, fire service, and emergency medical service;
b. Provide funding for education and public schools;
c. Finance or refinance infrastructure, including expenditures on road and bridge construction and maintenance and stormwater control;
d. Finance or refinance natural resource projects, including flood control measures;
e. Issue local bonds for uses consistent with this paragraph and to make debt service payments for existing obligations;
f. Meet obligations for retirement benefits of local government employees; or
g. Fund the operations and administration of county officers and commissioners established under Article VIII and municipalities, and the expenditures approved by such county officers or county or municipal governing bodies, except those expenditures prohibited by general law."

**Interpretive questions for counsel** (present them as questions, and don't answer them):
- **Breadth of (g).** Read literally, (g) allows ad valorem revenue for "the expenditures approved by such … county or municipal governing bodies, except those expenditures prohibited by general law." If that reading holds, the list may restrict very little until the Legislature enacts prohibitions. The ballot summary described the list as "core public needs." How a court weighs the summary against the text is an open question.
- **Parks, libraries, recreation, cultural facilities.** Possible routes: (b) "education" (libraries), (d) "natural resource projects" (parks, preserves, open space), (c) infrastructure (capital facilities), or (g). Which applies to which service is a judgment for counsel.
- **Special districts** are not named in § 9(a)(2). Their levies get the exemption but are not subject to the use limit.
- **General law.** Watch for implementing legislation under (g) "except those expenditures prohibited by general law" and under § 6(a)(4)a.1. Re-check the current session laws before relying on this section.

## 3. Assessment caps (Art. VII, § 4(g), (h))

For non-school levies, the annual cap on increases in assessed value for non-homestead residential property (§ 4(g)) and for all other non-homestead property (§ 4(h)) drops from 10% to 5% beginning January 1, 2027. Save Our Homes (homestead: lower of 3% or CPI) is unchanged. The 5% cap slows the growth of the remaining tax base. It doesn't cut today's base.

## 4. Ballot summary versus text

- The summary says the amendment "requires, through general law, a schedule for full elimination" of non-school homestead taxes. The text (§ 6(a)(4)a.1.) requires a uniform *procedure* by which counties and municipalities *may* increase their own exemption. It's a local option, not a statewide schedule.
- The summary describes "core public needs." The text includes the broad item (g) above.

## 5. Implementing law: CS/SB 4-F (Ch. 2026-240)

- Revises how the maximum millage that may be levied is calculated (s. 200.065) and the related definitions (s. 200.001(8)(l)-(m)) to conform to the amendment.
- Re-enacts the state appropriations that offset ad valorem revenue losses in *fiscally constrained counties* (ss. 218.12(2), 218.125(2), 218.136(2)). If the jurisdiction is fiscally constrained, include this offset in the gap analysis.
- Contains nothing that restricts non-ad valorem special assessments or fees.

## 6. Other 2026 legislation to check

- **Ch. 2026-37, Laws of Fla.** (HB 39 / CS/CS/SB 118, effective April 21, 2026, first applying to the 2026 roll) limits special assessments on recreational vehicle parks: they cannot reach campsite area beyond the maximum RV-unit square footage, and occupancy rates must be considered in apportionment (ss. 125.0168, 166.223, 189.052). If the jurisdiction has RV parks, apply this in the apportionment.
- The regular-session HJR 201 and HJR 203 (full homestead elimination; public safety maintenance-of-effort) died in committee.
- Re-check for 2027-session bills on assessments, fees, and the § 6(a)(4) procedure before advising.
