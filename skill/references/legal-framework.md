# Legal framework: fees, assessments and taxes

**What this file is for.** It sets out the tests and authorities so the user can apply them. It does not apply them. Whether a particular charge is a valid fee or assessment is the user's (counsel's) determination. Mark it `[COUNSEL DETERMINATION]` every time.

## 1. Why the line matters

- Local governments have no inherent taxing power. Ad valorem taxes come from Art. VII, § 9. "All other forms of taxation shall be preempted to the state except as provided by general law" (Art. VII, § 1(a)).
- *Collier County v. State*, 733 So. 2d 1012 (Fla. 1999): local governments "have no other authority to levy taxes, other than ad valorem taxes, except as provided by general law" ✓. A county "does, however, possess authority to impose special assessments and user fees" ✓.
- *City of Boca Raton v. State*, 595 So. 2d 25 (Fla. 1992): "a legally imposed special assessment is not a tax" ✓. Home rule lets a municipality act without specific legislative authorization ✓. (Its standard of review was later restated by *Sarasota Church of Christ*, see §3.2.)
- *State v. City of Port Orange*, 650 So. 2d 1 (Fla. 1994): calling a tax a "fee" doesn't change what it is. The court won't broaden taxing power "by semantics" ✓, and resolves doubt against the government (quoting *City of Tampa v. Birdsong Motors*) ✓.
- *Collier County*: "a change in the method of collection will not convert a prohibited tax into a valid fee" ✓.
- **Amendment 3 point:** the homestead exemption runs "except assessments for special benefits" (Art. VII, § 6(a)). A valid special assessment is collected from homesteads in full. A charge that fails as an assessment is a tax, and is then subject to the exemption and to preemption.

## 2. User fees

### 2.1 The test
*Port Orange*: user fees are "charges based upon the proprietary right of the governing body permitting the use of the instrumentality involved." They (1) are charged in exchange for a particular governmental service, (2) benefit the payer in a manner not shared by other members of society, and (3) are paid by choice: the payer can avoid the charge by not using the service ✓. Reaffirmed in *City of Gainesville v. State*, 863 So. 2d 138 (Fla. 2003) ✓.

- *Port Orange* held a "transportation utility fee" on developed property, used to fund road maintenance, to be an unauthorized tax ✓.
- **Voluntariness is not dispositive where a statute authorizes the utility.** *Gainesville*: "voluntariness of the fee is one factor to consider, it is not dispositive" ✓; where a statute authorizes a utility fee, the fee may be a user fee even though it is mandatory. *Gainesville* upheld a stormwater utility fee authorized by s. 403.0893 ✓.
- *Gainesville* also lists factors for telling a user fee from a special assessment: the name; how the amount relates to the value of the service; whether only users pay or all residents do; voluntariness; monthly versus one-time; cost recovery for improvements versus routine service; whether it is a traditional utility; whether a statute authorizes it as a fee. "None of these factors is controlling" ✓.

### 2.2 Fire "fees" and coercion
*Discount Sleep of Ocala, LLC v. City of Ocala*, 300 So. 3d 316 (Fla. 5th DCA 2020) (rev. denied per press reports, Nov. 2020): a fire-service "user fee" billed with utilities was an invalid tax. "The provision of fire service is not a utility" ✓. It is not specifically authorized by statute ✓. It funds a typical sovereign function ✓. The voluntariness prong failed because of disconnection, liens, and an "option" to move away, which amounted to "economic coercion" ✓. The court found the second prong satisfied. It treated the first two prongs as "more significant" (quoting *I-4 Commerce Ctr.*) ✓. The case concerns fire, not EMS.

**Practical meaning:** fire funding outside ad valorem has generally gone through the **special assessment** route (§3), not a "fee."

### 2.3 Revenue relative to cost
*Contractors & Builders Ass'n of Pinellas County v. City of Dunedin*, 329 So. 2d 314 (Fla. 1976), at n.5: "a modest surplus" over the cost of regulation, or "a modest profit" from a utility, does not turn the charge into a tax; "unreasonable reliance" on utility revenues does ✓. (Pin page cite: 317 n.5, inferred; confirm.)

### 2.4 Regulatory and impact fees (limited use as replacement revenue)
- Regulatory fees: limited to the cost of the regulation. Building permit fees are limited by s. 553.80(7).
- Impact fees: the dual rational nexus test, articulated in *Hollywood, Inc. v. Broward County*, 431 So. 2d 606 (Fla. 4th DCA 1983) (drawing on *Wald Corp.* and *Dunedin*) ✓: a reasonable connection between the need for capital facilities and the growth, and between the spending and the benefit to the payers. Funds must be earmarked ✓. Now codified in the Florida Impact Fee Act, s. 163.31801 (confirm the current subsection numbering; the nexus test has appeared at (3)(f)-(g) and (4)(f)-(g) in different versions). The government bears the burden of proof, without deference. *Bd. of Cnty. Comm'rs, Santa Rosa Cnty. v. Home Builders Ass'n of W. Fla.*, 325 So. 3d 981 (Fla. 1st DCA 2021) (per curiam affirmance of a temporary injunction), applies the special-benefit language of *Volusia County v. Aberdeen at Ormond Beach*, 760 So. 2d 126 (Fla. 2000). **Impact fees fund growth-related capital only. They cannot replace lost operating revenue.**

## 3. Special assessments

### 3.1 The two-prong test
*Boca Raton*: (1) the property assessed must derive a special benefit from the service or improvement, and (2) the assessment must be fairly and reasonably apportioned among the properties that receive the benefit ✓. Restated in *Morris v. City of Cape Coral*, 163 So. 3d 1174 (Fla. 2015) ✓.

**Special benefit = "logical relationship."** *Lake County v. Water Oak Management Corp.*, 695 So. 2d 667 (Fla. 1997): the test is not whether the benefit is "unique" or different from what the community receives, but whether there is a "logical relationship" between the services and the benefit to real property ✓. Benefits need not be direct or immediate, but must be substantial, certain, and capable of being realized within a reasonable time (excerpt).

### 3.2 Standard of review
*Sarasota County v. Sarasota Church of Christ, Inc.*, 667 So. 2d 180 (Fla. 1995): both prongs are "questions of fact for a legislative body rather than the judiciary" ✓. "The standard is the same for both prongs": the legislative determination should be upheld unless it is arbitrary ✓. The court disapproved earlier formulations ("palpably arbitrary," "reasonable people may differ") ✓. *Morris* (quoting *City of Winter Springs v. State*, 776 So. 2d 255, 261 (Fla. 2001)): "Even an unpopular decision, when made correctly, must be upheld" ✓.

**The record has to show the benefit.** *City of North Lauderdale v. SMM Properties, Inc.*, 825 So. 2d 343 (Fla. 2002), quoting *South Trail Fire Control Dist. v. State*, 273 So. 2d 380, 383 (Fla. 1973): a legislative body "cannot by its fiat make a special benefit to sustain a special assessment where there is no special benefit" ✓. "General findings" with no "competent, substantial evidence" of benefit to property will not stand ✓. Build the record with studies, data and testimony.

### 3.3 Services: what courts have accepted and rejected
| Service | Result | Authority |
|---|---|---|
| Fire protection | Special benefit to property (e.g. lower insurance premiums, enhanced value) | *Lake County* ✓; *Morris* ✓ |
| Solid waste disposal | Accepted | *Lake County* ✓ |
| Stormwater | Accepted (developed property generates the runoff the system treats) | *Sarasota Church of Christ* ✓ |
| Local capital improvements | Accepted on findings | *Boca Raton* ✓ |
| Comprehensive EMS | **Rejected.** EMS provides a "personal benefit to individuals," not to property. First-response medical aid that is part of fire service is distinguished | *North Lauderdale* ✓ |
| General law enforcement, courts, indigent health care | **Rejected.** "Cannot be the subject of a special assessment" | *Lake County*, at 670, quoted in *Collier County* ✓ |
| Sheriff, courts, libraries, parks, elections, public health, as an "interim governmental services fee" | **Rejected** as a tax under the user-fee analysis (payers not benefited in a way not shared by non-payers) | *Collier County* ✓ |

Note on precision: the "cannot be the subject of a special assessment" sentence in *Lake County* names law enforcement, courts and indigent health care. Libraries and parks enter through *Collier County*'s facts and fee analysis. Whether a particular library or park charge can be an assessment is `[COUNSEL DETERMINATION]`.

### 3.4 Apportionment
- Legislative function, reviewed under the arbitrary standard (*Sarasota Church of Christ* ✓).
- Tiered methods can be reasonable: *Morris* upheld a two-tier fire assessment, a flat Tier 1 charge for availability plus a Tier 2 charge on improved property keyed to the property appraiser's structure value, as a matter of first impression ✓, drawing on *Sarasota Church of Christ*'s approval of apportionment by developed property ✓.
- Purely value-based apportionment: the idea that a fire assessment cannot simply follow assessed value traces to *St. Lucie County-Fort Pierce Fire Prevention & Control District v. Higgs*, 141 So. 2d 744 (Fla. 1962), as explained in *Lake County* ("in proportion to its value"). *Morris* shows that a value-based factor within a tiered method can survive. `[COUNSEL DETERMINATION]` How much value-based weighting a given method can carry.
- Statutory limits: Ch. 2026-37 (RV parks and campgrounds, see `amendment3.md` §6). For county bonds issued after July 1, 2023, assessments securing them may not be levied on agricultural land classified under s. 193.461 (s. 125.01; confirm).
- Government-owned and exempt property: assessments generally reach exempt property, and drainage assessments reach government land (*Hobe-St. Lucie Conservancy Dist. v. Martin County*, 4th DCA 2021). But sovereign immunity barred a city from collecting stormwater *fees* from a school board (*Sch. Bd. of Miami-Dade Cnty. v. City of Miami Beach*, 3d DCA 2021). `[COUNSEL DETERMINATION]` for state and school parcels.

### 3.5 Finality
- *City of Cooper City v. Joliff*, 227 So. 3d 633 (Fla. 4th DCA 2017): a challenge to the apportionment of an otherwise lawful assessment attacks a voidable, not void, act. Short deadlines are enforced: courts have "consistently enforced" 10-, 20- and 30-day and three-month periods ✓. In *Joliff* the 20-day period was set in the **ordinance**, running from the commission's final action ✓. A party who does not "move in due time" waives the challenge (quoting *Hackney*, 1933) ✓. An assessment imposed "without jurisdiction" is void and not barred ✓. Followed in *Hobe-St. Lucie* (2021).
- Chapter 75 bond validation: *State Att'ys for 2d, 7th & 9th Jud. Cirs. v. Fla. PACE Funding Agency*, 424 So. 3d 478 (Fla. 2025): validation proceedings are statutory and sui generis. Rule 1.540 does not apply. A final validation judgment is "forever conclusive," and validity "shall never be called in question" (s. 75.09) ✓. Appeal is within 30 days (s. 75.08). A follow-on appeal, *Fla. PACE Funding Agency v. State*, SC2026-0440, was pending as of 2026, so check its status.

## 5. Questions to flag for counsel in every plan

1. Does each charge meet its test on *this* record? (special benefit + apportionment; or the user-fee prongs)
2. Is the record competent, substantial evidence (studies, data), not conclusory findings?
3. For fire: how is EMS cost separated out, and what share of medical response counts as first response within fire service?
4. Exempt, government and school parcels: assess, buy down, or exclude?
5. Any buy-down or hardship relief comes from non-assessment revenue. Does the plan fund it?
6. BIE: required, or exempt under s. 166.041(4)(c)?
7. Does § 9(a)(2) as amended limit the *remaining* ad valorem revenue in a way that changes which services need replacement funding?
8. Any later legislation or cases since this file's check date?
