# Replacement revenue options

This is a catalog of candidates. It doesn't decide anything. For each tool, the skill gathers the facts and the cost base, and **counsel decides** whether the charge holds up as a fee or assessment rather than a tax. Legal tests and authorities are in `legal-framework.md`.

How to use it: go down the list, drop what doesn't fit the jurisdiction, and for each one that remains fill in: cost base, what can't be recovered, apportionment method, sample rate, revenue, incidence, first fiscal year.

## A. Special assessments (collectible on the tax bill; not covered by the homestead exemption)

| Tool | Cost base | Common apportionment methods | Watch-outs (questions for counsel) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Fire protection / fire rescue assessment** | Fire department operating (and possibly capital) budget **minus** the share of cost attributable to emergency medical services | Usually tiered by property class (residential per dwelling unit; non-residential per square foot or per building, in ranges), with weights from historical call/demand data by class. A two-tier availability + improvement-value method was upheld in *Morris v. City of Cape Coral* | *North Lauderdale*: comprehensive EMS can't be assessed; first-response medical aid that is part of fire service may be. The record needs studies (call data, insurance/ISO, property protection), not conclusory findings. Apportionment purely by value is the ad valorem method *Water Oak* rejected. Exempt and government parcels: decide whether they are assessed or the cost is bought down from general funds. A buy-down for hardship or homesteads has to come from **other** revenue, not be shifted onto other payers |
| **Solid waste / residential collection & disposal** | Collection, disposal, recycling, landfill closure | Per dwelling unit (residential); by container/volume (commercial) | *Water Oak* (disposal). Is it already an enterprise fund on the utility bill? If so, it's cost recovery, not new revenue, unless the general fund now subsidizes it |
| **Stormwater** (as an assessment, or as a utility fee under s. 403.0893) | Stormwater operations, maintenance and capital | Equivalent Residential Unit (ERU) based on impervious area | *Sarasota Church of Christ*. If a stormwater fee already exists, the lever is the rate and the cost recovery (general fund salaries and work done for stormwater). Check that the rate study supports it |
| **Street lighting** | Energy, maintenance | Per parcel / front foot within the lighted area | Benefit area defined by geography |
| **Capital improvement assessments** (ch. 170 or home rule): roads, sidewalks, drainage, seawalls, undergrounding, downtown improvements | Specific capital project cost | Front footage, area, units, trips | Must benefit the assessed property (*Boca Raton*). Replaces *capital* spending, which frees ad valorem for operations |
| **Business improvement district / downtown assessment** | Downtown maintenance, security ambassadors, marketing | Per square foot / assessed value within the district | Watch for overlap with CRA tax increment |

Generally **not** assessable (*Collier County*, *Water Oak*, *North Lauderdale*; confirm with counsel): comprehensive EMS, general law enforcement, courts, libraries, parks and recreation programs, elections, public health, general administration. For these, look to fees (B) or to ad valorem under § 9(a)(2).

## B. User fees (voluntary use of a particular service)

| Area | Examples | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Recreation | Program and class fees, camps, aquatic admission/passes, tennis/pickleball court fees, athletic field rentals, tournament fees, non-resident differentials | Keep the rate reasonably related to the cost of the service (*Dunedin*). Cost recovery policy tiers (e.g. youth/community subsidized, adult/specialized near full cost) are a policy choice. Look at the incidence on lower-income residents |
| Facilities | Rentals of community centers, theaters and performing arts centers, pavilions and event space; event staffing and cost recovery | A theater can often recover more through rental rates, ticket surcharges and facility fees |
| Libraries | Non-resident cards, room rentals, printing, programs | State aid to public libraries is conditioned on providing free library service (see s. 257.17; verify the current text). `[COUNSEL DETERMINATION]` Check what the statute, any library cooperative agreement and grant terms limit |
| Parking | Meters, garages, permits | Proprietary |
| Public safety services delivered to a specific person | False alarm fees, special-event police/fire details, EMS transport billing (fee-for-service, not an assessment) | Transport billing is a common, defensible EMS revenue. Check the current rates and collection rates |
| Utilities (enterprise) | Water, wastewater, reclaimed, stormwater, solid waste | A reasonable return or payment-in-lieu-of-taxes transfer to the general fund: *Dunedin* allows a "modest" surplus and not "unreasonable reliance." `[COUNSEL DETERMINATION]` |

## C. Regulatory fees (cost of regulating)

Business/inspection programs, plan review, code compliance, fire inspections, right-of-way permits, special event permits. Limited to the **cost of the regulation**. Building permit fees are capped at the cost of enforcing the Building Code (s. 553.80(7)). Value: recover regulatory costs the general fund now pays for, which frees ad valorem.

## D. Cost recovery and reallocation (no new charge)

- Charge enterprise funds for staff who work on them. Indirect cost allocation plan.
- Interlocal reimbursement: county EMS funding, school resource officer cost sharing, fire/EMS service contracts with unincorporated areas.
- Grants, and state revenue sharing changes (watch for implementing legislation).
- Fiscally constrained county offsets (ss. 218.12, 218.125, 218.136), if the county qualifies.

## E. Taxes authorized by general law (not fees, so outside this skill's main lane, but list them)

The constitution allows these as taxes because general law authorizes them. Note them so the plan is complete:
- Municipal public service tax (s. 166.231, up to 10% on electricity, water, gas etc.), local communications services tax (ch. 202).
- Franchise fees (contractual, for right-of-way use).
- Local option fuel taxes (s. 336.025), discretionary sales surtaxes (s. 212.055), which require a referendum or county action, and many are county-level.
- Raising millage on the remaining base (capped at 10 mills; SB 4-F changes the maximum-millage calculation).

## F. Comparing against cuts

For every tool, show which cut on the government's list it would prevent, e.g. "Fire assessment at $X per residential unit offsets the $Y Fire Rescue reduction and keeps the [units/stations] in service." Decision-makers decide by service, not by mechanism.
