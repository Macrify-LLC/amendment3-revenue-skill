# Adoption and collection procedure

Checked 2026-11-04 against the Florida Statutes on leg.state.fl.us. Confirm the current text before relying on it, because these sections are amended often.
Counsel decides which vehicle applies. This file sets out the steps and deadlines for each.

## 1. Enacting the ordinance

**Municipalities: s. 166.041**
- Read by title or in full "on at least 2 separate days" (s. 166.041(3)(a)).
- Notice published once in a newspaper of general circulation "at least 10 days prior to adoption," with the contents the statute requires (s. 166.041(3)(a)).
- Majority of a quorum to enact. Effective 10 days after passage unless the ordinance says otherwise.
- **Business impact estimate (BIE)**, s. 166.041(4)(a): post it on the website no later than the date the notice is published. It must identify "any new charge or fee" on businesses. Exemptions are in s. 166.041(4)(c), including "ordinances relating to the adoption of budgets or budget amendments, including revenue sources necessary to fund the budget." `[COUNSEL DETERMINATION]` Whether a particular fee or assessment ordinance fits an exemption. The conservative course is to prepare a BIE anyway.
- After five years, a challenge for failure to follow s. 166.041 strictly is barred (s. 166.041(8)).

**Counties: s. 125.66**
- Notice of intent published at least 10 days before the meeting (Ch. 50). The BIE requirement is parallel (s. 125.66(3)). The ordinance takes effect on filing with the Department of State unless it says otherwise.

## 2. Special assessments: choosing the vehicle

- **Home-rule master ordinance** (common for service assessments such as fire, solid waste and stormwater): a master ordinance sets the procedure, and each year's initial and final assessment resolutions set the rate and roll. Authority: home-rule power (s. 166.021 / s. 125.01), the assessment being a non-tax exaction (*City of Boca Raton v. State*).
- **Chapter 170** (municipal capital improvements listed in s. 170.01): resolution under s. 170.03 → preliminary roll → 30 days' mailed notice plus two publications a week apart (s. 170.07) → hearing and equalization (s. 170.08) → confirmed roll recorded in the Improvement Lien Book.
- **County MSBU/MSTU** for unincorporated areas: s. 125.01(1)(q).
- `[COUNSEL DETERMINATION]` Which vehicle applies, and whether the jurisdiction's charter or code adds steps.

## 3. Collecting on the tax bill: the uniform method (s. 197.3632)

| Step | Deadline / requirement | Cite |
|---|---|---|
| Resolution of intent (first use, or a new assessment) | Adopt at a public hearing **before January 1**, or **before March 1** if the property appraiser, tax collector and local government agree | 197.3632(3)(a) |
| Notice of that hearing | Published **weekly for 4 consecutive weeks** before the hearing, in a newspaper of general circulation in each county | 197.3632(3)(a) |
| Send the adopted resolution | By US mail to the property appraiser, tax collector and Department of Revenue **by January 10** (or March 10 under the agreement) | 197.3632(3)(a) |
| Adopt the assessment roll | Public hearing **between January 1 and September 15** (Sept. 25 for s. 125.011(1) counties) | 197.3632(4)(a) |
| Notice of the roll hearing | **First-class mail to each owner at least 20 days before**, with the statutory contents (purpose, total amount against the parcel, unit of measurement, number of units, total revenue, the tax-certificate/loss-of-title warning, the right to appear and to file written objections within 20 days, hearing details), **plus** published notice | 197.3632(4)(b) |
| Certify the roll to the tax collector | **By September 15** (Sept. 25 for s. 125.011(1) counties) | 197.3632(5)(a) |
| Later years | Multi-year assessment: no annual re-adoption or re-notice unless the rate goes above the noticed maximum, the boundaries change, or the purpose changes | 197.3632(4)(a), (6) |
| Bill | Appears on the combined "Notice of Ad Valorem Taxes and Non-ad Valorem Assessments" | 197.3635 |

Practical points:
- The property appraiser and tax collector usually require an interlocal or service agreement (and charge for it, s. 197.3632(2)). Start that conversation early.
- Put a **maximum rate** in the initial notice, so that later increases up to it need no new mailed notice.
- Alternative: bill the assessment directly, or through the utility bill for utility-type services. That avoids the January 1 / March 1 deadline but loses tax-certificate enforcement. `[COUNSEL DETERMINATION]` Whether the charge holds up as a fee if billed that way (see *Discount Sleep of Ocala* in `legal-framework.md`).

## 4. Fees

- User and regulatory fees are usually set by ordinance or by resolution under an enabling ordinance. Follow the notice and BIE rules in §1 for the ordinance.
- **Building permit fees** are limited to the cost of enforcing the Florida Building Code, and the statute restricts carry-forward balances (s. 553.80(7)). They cannot fund general government.
- **Impact fees** (s. 163.31801) pay for growth-related capital only: 90 days' notice, data no more than 4 years old, caps on how fast they can rise, and the government bears the burden of proof. They cannot replace operating revenue.

## 5. Making it final

- Put a **short challenge period** in the final assessment resolution (courts have enforced 10-, 20- and 30-day and three-month periods for challenges to an apportionment that is merely voidable, see *City of Cooper City v. Joliff*).
- If the assessment secures bonds, a Chapter 75 validation action settles validity for good once the judgment is final.

## 6. Calendar builder (new assessment on the tax bill for the coming fiscal year)

Work backward from the bill date. Example: new assessment first billed November 2027 (FY 2027-28):

| Date | Action |
|---|---|
| ASAP | Authorize a methodology/cost study. Open talks with the property appraiser and tax collector for a March 1 agreement if January 1 can't be met |
| 4 weeks before the hearing | Begin the weekly published notice of intent (4 consecutive weeks) |
| Before Jan 1, 2027 (or Mar 1 with agreement) | Public hearing; adopt the resolution of intent |
| By Jan 10 (or Mar 10) | Mail the resolution to the property appraiser, tax collector and DOR |
| Spring 2027 | Finish the methodology report; adopt the master ordinance (2 readings, 10-day notice, BIE) |
| ~June-July 2027 | Initial assessment resolution sets the rate and directs notices |
| ≥20 days before the hearing | Mail first-class notices and publish |
| By Sept 15, 2027 | Hearing; final assessment resolution adopts the roll; certify to the tax collector |
| Nov 2027 | Assessment appears on the tax bill |

If even the March 1 date can't be met, the choices are: (a) bill directly or through a utility bill in FY 2027-28 and move to the tax bill in FY 2028-29, or (b) start in FY 2028-29, which is when the full $250,000 hits.
