AMENDMENT 3

The new homestead exemption, and a way for your city or county to work through it.

A free AI skill for Florida local governments. Give it your adopted budget and the state tax roll. It measures the revenue you stand to lose, goes through your programs with you, lays out options to evaluate, from trimming or redesigning programs to special assessments and user fees, and drafts the documents for your attorney to review.

Options a city or county could consider

  • Redesign, trim or stop programs
  • Special assessments, such as fire, stormwater or solid waste
  • User fees for services people choose to use
  • Other revenue, or living with less

Assessments and fees aren't covered by the homestead exemption. None of them happens automatically: each needs a study, public hearings, and review by the government's attorney.

What changed

Most homesteads owe far less city and county tax

The homestead exemption from non-school property taxes rises to $150,000 of assessed value in 2027 and $250,000 in 2028, then grows with inflation from 2029. A homestead assessed at $250,000 or less will owe no city or county property tax in 2028. School taxes are not affected.

Art. VII, § 6(a), as amended by HJR 1-F. People who move to Florida after 2026 get a $50,000 exemption for their first five years. For businesses, rentals and other non-homestead property, the yearly cap on increases in assessed value for non-school taxes drops from 10% to 5% (Art. VII, § 4).

Property tax may only pay for certain things

The amendment lists what remaining property tax revenue can be used for. One item is broad, so how much this limits a given program is a question for the government's attorney.

Art. VII, § 9(a)(2), as amended.

Assessments and fees work differently

The exemption doesn't reach "assessments for special benefits," and user fees aren't taxes. So a government can ask property owners or users to pay for specific services instead, if each charge meets Florida's legal tests. That moves some of the cost back onto homesteads, and the skill shows who pays more and who pays less.

Art. VII, § 6(a); read the enrolled text.

How the skill works

It's a long conversation, because these are big decisions. At the end of each stage it writes a short note on what was found and decided, so anyone can pick up where you left off.

  1. MILE1

    Start

    Who you are, which government, what documents you have, and the deadlines that still work this year.

  2. MILE2

    Measure the loss

    Reads your budget and the state tax roll, parcel by parcel, and checks the result against your own estimate.

  3. MILE3

    Go through your programs

    Flags programs that may fall outside the new property-tax uses. You decide: keep, redesign, fund another way, shrink or stop.

  4. MILE4

    Compare packages

    Two or three combinations of choices and charges, with what typical homes and businesses would pay under each.

  5. MILE5

    Draft the documents

    Resolutions, ordinances, notices and studies as Word files, with as many blanks filled in as the record allows.

  6. MILE6

    Write the plan

    A plain-language memo of the choices, the trade-offs, the calendar and every open question.

You make the calls

The skill supplies ideas, numbers and drafts. The judgment stays with the people accountable for it.

Legality is your attorney's call

It lays out the legal tests and the record for each charge, and marks every legal question for counsel. It never says a charge is valid.

Priorities are yours

It won't rank programs or pick a package unless you ask, and then it says what the other choices give up.

Every number has a source

Each figure cites the budget page, tax roll field or document it came from. Anything it can't find goes on a list of questions for staff.

Every decision is written down

A decision log records what you chose, what else was on the table, and why, in your words.

    I assumed you mean the City of Largo, since that's your folder's name, and the city's own parcels in your tax roll file confirm it. Largo expects to lose about $15.7 million a year once Amendment 3 is fully in effect.

    I also rebuilt the city's part myself from the 2026 state tax roll … About 12,100 of 15,800 homesteads (77%) would pay no city property tax at all.

    The skill at work: it checks its numbers against the city's own estimate, starts with the public-records warning, and asks before deciding anything.
  4. 9 min

Get the skill

Free and open source under the MIT license. It's a folder of instructions, legal references, document templates and small Python scripts that an AI assistant follows.

Download the skill (.zip) View the source on GitHub

Version 0.1.0-rc2, a release candidate (release notes and past versions). In Claude, add the zip under Settings, then Capabilities, then Skills.

Before you start: in Florida, a chat about government business is likely a public record. Keep it, and leave out anything confidential or privileged. Board members shouldn't share or discuss the chat with each other outside a noticed meeting.

What you need

  • An AI assistant that can use the files and run Python. The skill was built and tested in Claude, where you add the folder as a skill. Other assistants that accept uploaded files and can run code should be able to follow it. The skill is free, but your assistant may need a paid plan.
  • Your adopted budget, and any staff estimate of the Amendment 3 impact.
  • The state tax roll file for your county from the Florida Department of Revenue's data portal. It's large, so the skill can cut it down to just your parcels.
  • Time. Plan on a long conversation, over more than one sitting.

This is not legal advice. The skill and its drafts are starting points. A Florida-licensed attorney must review every legal question and every document before anything is adopted. The legal references were checked on the dates shown in each file, and the law may change.
